03

The three scores

Return · risk · readiness
The scoring

Every candidate use-case gets three scores. Return: hours or pounds, measured against what the workflow costs today. Risk: what happens when the AI is wrong, and who is accountable when it is. Readiness: whether the data, the permissions and the tenant can actually support it. The shortlist is ranked on all three.

The use-cases that fail are written down too, with the reason. The board should know what was considered and why it lost. And where the readiness score is the blocker, the roadmap starts with the groundwork: a tenant made ready, the evidence pack, or waste in the bill reclaimed to fund the work.

07

FAQs

Asked before, answered here

What do we get at the end of the AI Opportunity Audit?

A board-readable pack: where the hours and the money go today, a shortlist of AI use-cases scored on return, risk and data readiness, the groundwork check on security and licensing, and a sequenced roadmap with the first build costed.

How long does the audit take?

Two to three weeks elapsed. We sit with the people who run the work, read the estate, and score what we find. It is deliberately short: the point is a decision.

Do we need to have bought Copilot or any AI tools first?

No. The audit is tool-independent and vendor-independent. We sell no licences and take no vendor margin. If the right answer is to buy nothing yet, the roadmap says so.

Will you just tell us to hire you to build things?

The roadmap prices the first build honestly, and you can take it to anyone. If a workflow is better served by an off-the-shelf product, or by your MSP, the pack names that instead.

How do you know if our data is ready for AI?

Readiness is one of the three scores. AI is only as good as what it can see, and what it must never see. The audit checks ownership, retention, labels and access before recommending that anything switches on.

What happens after the audit?

Your call. Some firms take the roadmap and run it themselves. Some take the first build as a production agent sprint or Copilot readiness. Some fill the Head of IT seat. The audit stands alone either way.
