FAQs

Asked before, answered here

Do we have to keep the seat after the first sprint?

No. When the first sprint ends you choose: keep the seat at one or two days a week, or take the gains in-house. Nothing obliges you to continue, and the firm is stronger either way.

How long does each piece of work take?

Every piece of work is a bounded sprint, with the scope fixed during the diagnosis. The AI Opportunity Audit is the shortest, two to three weeks, because its job is to tell you where AI pays before you spend.

Who do you talk to first?

The people who actually run the estate: the MSP lead, finance, operations, whoever holds the passwords. Then the FD or MD who started the conversation.

Will you present to the board?

Yes. Every piece of work ends in something a board can read. That is what the sprint is for.

Do you work on site?

When the estate needs it. Much of the work is remote: in the tenant, in the billing, and in the documents that prove the controls. The phone number is a Bristol number. The registered office is in London.

Is this IT support?

No. You keep the MSP if the MSP works. We sit above the helpdesk, on the decisions: the bill, the risk, the AI question, the board answer.

Can two services run in the same sprint?

Sometimes. The seat pairs naturally with any of the sprints, and the audit often comes first because it tells you which sprint pays. We will not pile every sprint on a firm that needs one.
