Who it is for

The board asked. The tenant is still open.

This is for the firm that bought Copilot, or is about to, and still has a shared drive that would fail an insurer. The licences sit on the bill. Nobody wants to be the person who switched them on.

Professional services and wealth practices feel this hardest. Client files, historic folders, and a tenant that grew by habit.

If the board asked about Copilot and the room went quiet, this is the piece of work.

The engagement

Clean the tenant. Then thirty days.

We start with Purview, oversharing, sensitivity labels, and the seats. The job is to stop Copilot reading the wrong file. That is the readiness.

Then a 30-day pilot on the seats you actually need. We do not dump licences across the firm.

At the end the board has a clear answer: who has a seat, what the tenant will allow, and whether the pilot earned the next thirty days.

What you leave with

A tenant that can hold Copilot.

  • The right seats

    A 30-day pilot on the people who will actually use it.

  • A board answer

    Who is live, what is still blocked, and what the next month costs.

Questions an FD asks

Before you book.

The licences are already on the bill. Why wait?

Because Copilot stays unsafe while the shared drive is. Switching on a dirty tenant is how client files end up in a prompt.

What does oversharing mean in a 120-person firm?

Folders that everyone can see, Teams that outlived the project, and guest access nobody reviewed. That is the usual picture.

Who should get a seat?

The people with a job the pilot can measure. The rest wait.

Will Copilot see client files?

That is the question the readiness answers. Until labels and sharing are under control, assume yes.

What happens after the 30-day pilot?

You keep the seats that earned it. You drop the ones that did not. The Head of IT retainer can own the next quarter if the seat is still empty.

Do you train the whole firm?

The work is the tenant and a 30-day pilot. Training follows the seats that stay.
